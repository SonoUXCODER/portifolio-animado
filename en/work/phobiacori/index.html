PHOBIACORI

E-commerce / Digital Product / 2026

PHOBIACORI has been drawing strange creatures in ink since 2019 and selling them one direct message at a time. The store had to fit that way of working: small runs, packed by hand, nothing resembling a conveyor belt.

The challenge

What wasbroken.

Selling art in small runs has nothing to do with running a generic shop. The catalogue changes every week, half the pieces are one of a kind, and a marketplace layout made her work look like factory stock. On top of that: no budget for a backend, and no appetite for a platform that takes a cut of every sale.

The approach

How itwas made.

  1. Research

    Reading two years of direct messages

    Before any interface, I read how she was already selling. Buyers never asked for a size chart. They asked whether the piece was still available and how it would be packed. That single finding decided the whole information hierarchy.

  2. UX Strategy

    A catalogue, not a shop window

    Each piece became a record card: the drawing large, the text next to it, availability stated in plain language. Buying is three taps and never asks who you are. The cart lives in local storage and the order leaves as a written message.

  3. Wireframes

    One page, two jobs

    Store and archive share a single scroll. Splitting them into separate routes tested worse: people came to look, and only decided to buy after looking. Sold-out work stays visible as archive instead of disappearing.

  4. UI Design

    Photocopy, tape, ink

    The interface borrows the material language of the work itself: paper white, ink black, one red for alerts. Nothing is centred, nothing is rounded, and every image sits slightly off the grid on purpose.

  5. Development

    Static, and therefore permanent

    Next.js with a static export on GitHub Pages. There is no server to go down, no monthly bill, and no database to migrate. The product list is a typed data file she edits herself.
