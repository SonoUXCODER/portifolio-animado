Truffle N.B.

Catalogue / Seasonal Product / 2025

Fresh truffle lasts days, not months. The site had to say what exists today and how long it takes to arrive, and nothing beyond that, because everything beyond that goes out of date faster than anyone can edit it.

↳designing for content that ages by itself changed how I think about shelf life.

2025
Role

Disciplines
UX/UI / FRONTEND
Status
Live

The challenge

What wasbroken.

Seasonal produce ages on screen. A static page still advertising a truffle that ran out three weeks ago is worse than having no page at all. It costs trust, and trust is the entire product when someone is spending CHF 200 on something they cannot see.

The approach

How itwas made.

  1. Research

    How the season actually moves

    Three varieties, three windows, and a delivery radius that changes with the calendar. I mapped the whole year before drawing anything, because the calendar is the real information architecture here.

  2. UX Strategy

    Availability is the top-level filter

    The page opens on what is in season right now. Everything else is one scroll below, marked with the month it returns. Out of stock becomes information rather than a dead end.

  3. Wireframes

    Three cards and a delivery line

    The whole catalogue is short by design. Adding filters, sorting and a search field to nine products would be interface for its own sake.

  4. UI Design

    Earth, rust, linen

    Colours taken from the product itself. Photography runs large and uncropped; type stays small and quiet, so nothing competes with what is being sold.

  5. Development

    The catalogue reads a data file

    React and Vite, with the product list in a typed file the client edits. Anything out of season drops off the list and the delivery copy changes with it. The site expires correctly, on its own.
